4. **The mixed-degree argument requires an explicit subcubic hypothesis; equality alone does not exclude chorded squares.** \(W_5\) itself is an equality graph containing chorded squares. Thus the proof must first discharge the degree-four case and then assume \(\Delta(G)\le3\). The longer reconstruction names this restriction, but the compressed statements can easily lose it.

   Under that restriction, the alleged mixed-case failure does not materialize. The indispensable dependencies are:

   - If \(t=a\), a square using \(uv\) must be \(u,v,b,a,u\), with chord \(va\). Thus excluding chorded squares forces \(a,b,t\) distinct.
   - Distinctness means every surviving vertex loses at most one edge. Original minimum degree two then excludes residual isolates.
   - The three cut edges force one residual component, because each component needs at least two **cut edges**.
   - Only after proving residual tightness and non-isolation can square support imply residual degree at least two. Subcubicity then gives attachment degree exactly two.

   \{v,t,s\}\ \sqcup\ \{u,a,b\},

5. **Two tempting attachment/tightness shortcuts are false.**

   First, two attachment **edges** need not have distinct residual endpoints. Take the two squares
   \[
   (0,1,2,3,0),\qquad(0,4,5,6,0).
   \]
   Delete the connected set \(\{1,2,3\}\). The residual component has two attachment edges, \(01\) and \(03\), both incident with residual vertex \(0\). This is an explicit counterexample to an overstrong attachment lemma, although the reconstruction correctly asks for edge counting.

   Second, a tight square-supported graph can have isolates: \(C_4\sqcup K_1\). Residual tightness therefore cannot replace the separate non-isolation arguments.

   Componentwise tightness must follow from
   \[
   4\tau(G)\le4c+4\tau(H)\le4c+m(H)=m(G)
   \]
   and then
   \[
   \sum_C\bigl(m(C)-4\tau(C)\bigr)=0,
   \]
   with each summand nonnegative by A. Equality components with zero edges must not be fed into B.

   In the degree-four/two-component case, distinct attachment endpoints follow from simplicity because all cut edges originate at the single deleted vertex. A square through that vertex leaves a three-vertex path in one residual component, so it uses both attachments there. The argument needs this path observation and the prescribed-vertex transversal property; attachment counting alone does not suffice.

6. **The cubic “mixed square” assertion needs an explicit argument about crossings.** A cycle crosses between the two squares an even number of times. Four crossings in a four-cycle would require consecutive matching edges at a vertex, impossible for a matching. Therefore a mixed square has exactly two crossings. Those matching edges cannot be consecutive, so its other two edges are one internal edge in each square.

   One further implication is suppressed: if the first internal edge contains \(c\), the square also contains its matched vertex \(\mu(c)\). That is what makes \(\{a,\mu(c)\}\) a transversal when \(a,c\) are opposite—not merely the observation that every first-square edge meets \(\{a,c\}\).

   I checked all 24 matching permutations. None supplies a counterexample to this assertion or the proposed transversal.

7. **The chorded-square reduction must specify the diagonal and the order of deletion.** Given diagonal \(ac\) in \(abcd a\), delete tip \(b\). Subcubicity saturates \(a,c\), so the remaining triangle \(acd\) can meet the outside only at \(d\). A simple square cannot enter and leave that triangle through the same sole attachment vertex. Its three edges are therefore sterile **after** deleting \(b\).

   The removed-edge count is exactly \(d_G(b)+3\ge5\). This also handles \(K_4\) by choosing either diagonal. Applying this reduction at maximum degree four would be invalid.

   The five reductions exhaust the possibilities only after restricting to a component containing an edge and removing unsupported edges. Otherwise isolated vertices invalidate the stated degree dichotomy. For an active square-supported component, minimum degree is two; after excluding degree at least four, a path between the two degree classes supplies an adjacent \(2/3\) pair whenever degrees are not constant.
